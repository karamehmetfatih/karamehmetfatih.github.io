I am a plant scientist working on disease resistance in lettuce, with a focus on how the crop responds to necrotrophic fungal pathogens at the transcriptional level. I am a PhD researcher at the University of York.

My current work combines time-series transcriptomics, reference transcriptome construction and CRISPR-Cas9 genome editing to identify and test candidate regulators of resistance — reading that response at the level of individual transcripts rather than whole genes. Before moving into plant pathology and molecular biology, I worked in seed science on the longevity, storage and priming of vegetable seed.

Research

Plant–pathogen interactions. How lettuce (Lactuca sativa) defends itself against necrotrophic fungi such as Botrytis cinerea and Sclerotinia sclerotiorum, and which regulators of that response could be useful in breeding for resistance.

Transcriptomics. Building transcript-level resources for lettuce, including LsRTDv1, a reference transcript dataset that makes accurate transcript-specific expression analysis possible in this crop.

Genome editing. Applying CRISPR-Cas9 approaches to functionally test candidate resistance genes in lettuce.

Seed science. Earlier work on seed longevity, storage and priming in lettuce, onion and cabbage.
